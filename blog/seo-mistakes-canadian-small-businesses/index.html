5 SEO Mistakes Canadian Small Businesses Make (And How to Fix Them)

Kazi Hasnain Founder & CEO, ReformedTech.ca

Most small business websites across BC and Canada leave significant ranking opportunities on the table — not because of complex technical issues, but because of a handful of very common, very fixable mistakes. Here is what to check first.

1. Trying to rank one page for everything

Many small business sites have a homepage and a single “Services” page that lists everything they do. Google then has no clear page to show for any specific search, so the site ranks weakly for all of them.

How to spot it

Search for your main service plus your city (for example “bathroom renovation Kelowna”). If no single page on your site is clearly about that topic, this is you.

How to fix it

  • Give each core service its own page, with one main search phrase per page.
  • Put that phrase in the page title, the H1 and the first paragraph, written naturally.
  • Link the service pages to each other and from your homepage.

2. Neglecting your Google Business Profile

For local searches, your Google Business Profile often matters as much as your website. An incomplete profile with the wrong category, old hours or no recent reviews makes it much harder to appear in the map results.

How to fix it

  • Choose the most specific primary category for your main service.
  • Complete every section: services, service areas, hours, photos and description.
  • Ask every happy customer for a review and reply to all of them.
  • Make sure your business name, address and phone number match your website exactly.

3. A slow website that’s hard to use on a phone

Most local searches happen on mobile. If your site is slow, the text is tiny or buttons are hard to tap, visitors leave, and Google uses page experience as part of how it evaluates sites.

How to spot it

Open your site on your own phone using mobile data, not Wi-Fi. Then run your key pages through Google’s free PageSpeed Insights and check the Core Web Vitals results.

How to fix it

  • Compress and resize images, which are the most common cause of slow pages.
  • Remove plugins, sliders and scripts you don’t need.
  • Make phone numbers tap-to-call and keep buttons large and easy to tap.
  • If your site is built on an old theme or template, a rebuild can be cheaper than patching it repeatedly.

4. Thin, copied or city-swapped content

Pages with only a few lines of text, descriptions copied from manufacturers or competitors, and dozens of near-identical city pages give Google little reason to rank you.

How to fix it

  • Answer the questions customers actually ask you: cost, timelines, process and what’s included.
  • Rewrite copied text in your own words, using your own experience.
  • Merge weak, similar pages into fewer, stronger ones.
  • Open key pages with a direct two- or three-sentence answer. This also helps you appear in AI answers (see what AEO means for small businesses).

5. Not measuring anything

Without tracking, you can’t tell which pages bring in leads, which searches you show up for, or whether your SEO spend is working.

How to fix it

  • Set up Google Search Console (free) to see the searches you appear for and your clicks.
  • Set up Google Analytics 4 and mark form submissions and phone clicks as key events.
  • Check your Google Business Profile performance for calls and direction requests.
  • Ask every new lead how they found you, and write it down.

Bonus: forgetting you’re a Canadian business

This one is easy to miss. Watch for:

  • Templates written for the US: American spelling, “zip code” fields, US phone formats or prices without “CAD”.
  • No clear service area. Say which cities and regions you serve, such as BC, the Okanagan or Metro Vancouver.
  • No Canadian trust signals, such as local reviews, a BC address, industry associations or local projects.

Quick self-check. If you can’t answer “yes” to all five, start with the first “no”: (1) Does each main service have its own page? (2) Is your Google Business Profile complete and current? (3) Does your site load quickly on a phone? (4) Is your content original and specific? (5) Can you see which pages bring in leads?

Frequently asked questions

Which SEO mistake should I fix first?
For most local service businesses, start with your Google Business Profile and a dedicated page for each main service. These usually have the biggest effect for the least effort.
Can I fix these SEO mistakes myself?
Many of them, yes, especially your Google Business Profile, reviews and tracking. Site speed, page structure and technical fixes are often quicker with a developer, depending on how your site was built.
How long after fixing these will I see results?
It depends on your market and how competitive your searches are. Profile and technical fixes can show movement within weeks; content and reviews build over months.
