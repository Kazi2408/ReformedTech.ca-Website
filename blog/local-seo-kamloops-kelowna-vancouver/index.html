The No-Fluff Guide to Local SEO for Small Businesses in Kamloops, Kelowna, and Vancouver

Local SEO is the process of optimizing your online presence so that people in your geographic area — Kamloops, Kelowna, or Vancouver — find your business when they search for what you offer. It covers your Google Business Profile, consistent business citations, customer reviews, and location-specific website content. For BC small businesses, local SEO is typically the fastest path to more phone calls and booked appointments.

Kazi Hasnain Founder & CEO, ReformedTech.ca

Local SEO isn’t complicated, but it is easy to do in the wrong order. This guide covers the steps that matter most for service businesses in Kamloops, Kelowna and Vancouver, in the order we’d tackle them, plus how each market changes the plan.

What local SEO actually covers

When someone searches “plumber near me” or “Kelowna bookkeeper”, Google usually shows three places your business can appear:

  • The map pack: the map and three business listings near the top, driven mostly by your Google Business Profile.
  • The organic results: the regular links below, driven by your website.
  • AI answers: summaries from Google’s AI Overviews and tools like ChatGPT, which draw on both. See our guide to AEO for Canadian small businesses.

Good local SEO improves all three at once.

Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

For most local service businesses, this is the single highest-impact task. Log in at business.google.com and check:

  • Primary category: choose the most specific option that describes your main service (for example “Roofing contractor”, not “Contractor”). Add secondary categories only if they’re genuinely relevant.
  • Service areas: list the communities you actually serve, such as Kamloops, Sun Peaks and Chase, or Kelowna, West Kelowna and Lake Country.
  • Services and descriptions: add each service with a short, plain-English description.
  • Hours, phone and website: keep them accurate, including holiday hours.
  • Photos: real photos of your team, work and premises build far more trust than stock images.
  • Posts: share updates, offers or recent projects regularly so the profile looks active.

Step 2: Make your business details consistent everywhere

Google cross-checks your name, address and phone number (often called NAP) across the web. Small differences, such as “Suite 200” on one site and “#200” on another, or an old phone number, create doubt.

  1. Pick one exact format for your business name, address and phone number.
  2. Use it on your website footer and contact page.
  3. Update the main listings: Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yellow Pages Canada, the Better Business Bureau, and your local chamber of commerce or industry association.
  4. Search your business name and old phone numbers to find and fix outdated listings.

Step 3: Build a steady stream of reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people choose you once they find you.

  • Ask at the right moment: right after a job is finished and the customer is happy.
  • Make it easy: send a direct review link by text or email.
  • Reply to every review, including negative ones, calmly and specifically.
  • Encourage detail: reviews that mention the service and the area (“fixed our furnace in Aberdeen”) are more useful to customers and to search engines.

Don’t buy reviews or offer incentives for them. Google’s policies prohibit fake and incentivized reviews, and violations can lead to removed reviews or profile restrictions.

Step 4: Create location pages that aren’t spam

If you serve several cities, give each important market its own page, but only if you can make it genuinely different. Copying one page and swapping the city name is easy for Google to spot and does little for you.

A useful location page includes:

  • The services you offer in that city and any differences in availability or pricing.
  • Neighbourhoods or nearby communities you serve.
  • Local projects, photos or customer reviews from that area.
  • Local details that matter to the job, such as permits, climate or building types.
  • A clear call to action with the right phone number and service area.

How the plan changes in Kamloops, Kelowna and Vancouver

MarketWhat’s differentWhere to focus
KamloopsSmaller market; many local categories have only a handful of businesses actively doing SEO.A complete, active Google Business Profile and steady reviews can move you up quickly. Cover nearby Thompson-Nicola communities you serve.
KelownaGrowing Okanagan market with seasonal demand in tourism, hospitality and home services.Plan content and Google posts around the season. Be clear about West Kelowna, Lake Country and other areas you cover.
VancouverDense, highly competitive market, and Metro Vancouver is made up of many separate cities.Target specific cities and neighbourhoods (Burnaby, North Vancouver, Kitsilano), build local links and invest in standout content.

Step 5: Cover the technical basics

  • Mobile-first: most local searches happen on phones, so test every key page on a phone.
  • Speed: run your pages through Google’s free PageSpeed Insights and fix the biggest issues it flags.
  • Click-to-call: make your phone number a tappable link.
  • LocalBusiness schema: add structured data that states your name, address, phone, hours and service area.

Step 6: Earn local links and mentions

Links from relevant local websites signal that you’re an established part of the community. Good sources include chambers of commerce, industry associations, suppliers and partners, local charity or sports sponsorships, and coverage in local news.

How to track progress

  • Google Business Profile performance: calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches that found you.
  • Google Search Console: which local searches show your website, and your clicks over time.
  • Leads: ask every new enquiry how they found you, and track calls and form submissions as conversions.

Expect local SEO to build over weeks and months rather than days. Fixing your profile and listings often shows the earliest movement; reviews, content and links keep compounding after that.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a physical address in a city to rank there?
Not always. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the areas they serve. You’ll generally find it easiest to rank in the map pack close to your actual location, and use your website’s location pages to compete further out.
How many reviews do I need?
There’s no fixed number. Compare yourself with the businesses currently in the map pack for your main search, then aim for a steady flow of recent, detailed reviews rather than a one-off burst.
Should I create a separate page for every city I serve?
Only for cities where you have real work, customers or local information to share. A few strong, genuinely local pages beat dozens of near-identical ones.
Is local SEO different from regular SEO?
It uses the same foundations, but adds location signals: your Google Business Profile, consistent listings, local reviews, local links and location-specific content.
