What Is AEO and Why Canadian Small Businesses Can’t Ignore It in 2026

AEO — Answer Engine Optimisation — is the practice of structuring your content so AI tools like Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity surface your business as the direct answer to customer questions. Unlike traditional SEO, which targets blue-link rankings, AEO targets the AI-generated summaries that now appear before organic results. For Canadian small businesses in 2026, being cited in these AI answers is increasingly the difference between getting found and getting skipped.

Kazi Hasnain Founder & CEO, ReformedTech.ca

If you run a small business in Canada, your customers are changing how they search. More people now type, or say, a full question like “Who does emergency furnace repair in Kamloops?” and get an answer written by an AI tool rather than a list of ten links. AEO is how you make sure your business is part of that answer.

AEO vs SEO: what’s actually different?

AEO doesn’t replace SEO. It builds on it. Answer engines still rely on well-built, crawlable websites, but they reward a different kind of page: one that answers a specific question clearly and can be quoted.

Why AEO matters for Canadian small businesses now

  • Answers appear before the clicks. When an AI summary answers the question at the top of the page, fewer people scroll to the regular results. If you’re not in the summary, you can be invisible even with a decent ranking.
  • Local questions are a natural fit. “Best accountant in Kelowna for small business” or “Does anyone in Vancouver install heat pumps on weekends?” are exactly the kinds of questions answer engines try to resolve, and they lean on local business data to do it.
  • Early movers set the reference points. AI tools tend to repeat the sources they already trust. Many smaller Canadian markets still have few businesses with clear, well-structured content, which leaves room to become the reference.

How answer engines decide who to mention

No AI company publishes an exact formula, but the patterns are consistent. Answer engines favour sources that are:

  1. Clear. The page states the answer plainly, near the top, in a sentence or two that can be lifted as-is.
  2. Structured. Descriptive headings, short paragraphs, lists, tables and FAQs make it easy to pull the right fact.
  3. Consistent. Your business name, address, phone number, services and service area match across your website, Google Business Profile and directories.
  4. Corroborated. Reviews, mentions on other reputable sites and local press confirm you are who you say you are.
  5. Accessible. The content is in plain HTML that crawlers can read, not hidden in images, PDFs or scripts.

A 7-step AEO checklist you can start this month

  1. List the questions your customers actually ask. Pull them from phone calls, emails, sales conversations and Google Search Console. Aim for 20 to 30.
  2. Answer each one directly. Open every service page and blog post with a two- or three-sentence answer before you add detail, like the summary at the top of this article.
  3. Use question-style headings. “How much does a kitchen renovation cost in Kelowna?” is easier to match than “Our Pricing”.
  4. Add an FAQ section to key pages. Keep answers short, factual and specific to your business and location.
  5. Add structured data. LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage and Article schema help machines understand who you are and what each page covers.
  6. Fix your business details everywhere. Make your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and Canadian directories match your website exactly.
  7. Earn real reviews and mentions. Ask happy customers for reviews that mention the service and the city, and look for local associations, partners and media that can reference you.

Avoid shortcuts. Mass-produced AI pages, keyword-stuffed FAQs and paid or fake reviews tend to backfire. Search engines and AI tools are built to filter out low-trust sources, and fake reviews break Google’s policies.

How to tell whether AEO is working

  • Ask the engines yourself. Once a month, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google the same 10 customer questions and note whether, and how, your business appears.
  • Check AI referral traffic. In Google Analytics 4, look for referral sessions from sources such as chatgpt.com and perplexity.ai.
  • Watch question-style queries. Google Search Console shows which longer, question-based searches are bringing impressions and clicks.
  • Ask new leads how they found you. Add “AI assistant (ChatGPT, etc.)” as an option on your contact form.

Frequently asked questions

Is AEO replacing SEO?
No. AEO depends on SEO foundations: a fast, crawlable website, relevant content and trustworthy business information. Think of AEO as the next layer on top, focused on being quoted in AI answers rather than only ranking as a link.
Can I pay to appear in ChatGPT or Google’s AI Overviews?
You can’t buy a place in the organic AI answer itself. You earn it by publishing clear, accurate content and keeping your business information consistent and well reviewed across the web.
How long does AEO take to show results?
It varies by industry, location and how much useful content you already have. Fixing business details and adding direct answers to key pages are quick wins; building authority through reviews and mentions takes longer and compounds over time.
Can a small business do AEO without an agency?
Yes. The checklist above is a solid starting point. An agency helps when you want structured data, a content plan and ongoing tracking handled for you, or when you’re in a competitive market.
